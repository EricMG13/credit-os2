## Findings

### Bottom line
TransDigm's high reported profitability supports debt servicing, but the available peers do not establish superior credit quality or justify a valuation premium: different business models, earnings definitions and reporting durations prevent a defensible leverage ranking or sector range.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Research questions (CP-DR)

StandardAero supplies a usable, but limited, profitability observation; HEICO supplies operating corroboration and selected financial operands, not a comparable EBITDA measure. StandardAero's materials pass-through depresses its revenue margin structurally, while TransDigm's reported and adjusted earnings are themselves different measures. Creditors should therefore distinguish operating resilience from leverage tolerance. The strongest contrary evidence is independent aftermarket demand strength: it would support a more favorable relative credit interpretation if matched-period cash conversion and retained earnings demonstrated sustained debt reduction. Those comparisons are not established.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 71; Financial foundation (CP-1); Research questions (CP-DR)

### Credit interpretation and challenge
The apparent profitability gap is principally a comparability warning, not a favorable outlier verdict. StandardAero passes OEM materials through with little or no mark-up, enlarging revenue without proportionate earnings. TransDigm's engineered-component economics and separately disclosed earnings adjustments make a direct spread in margins misleading. The creditor consequence is narrower than an operating-quality ranking: TransDigm has a substantial earnings buffer before interest, but this evidence cannot establish how much more leverage that buffer safely supports than at peers.
Basis: web-912e342c5634, page 3; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 71; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The strongest counterargument is operating rather than valuation based. HEICO's Flight Support growth and StandardAero's maintenance demand corroborate aftermarket resilience across separate company disclosures. That reduces reliance on TransDigm's own demand narrative. However, HEICO also reports adjacent electronics weakness and StandardAero describes parts constraints. The positive case would prevail if sustained aftermarket activity translated into collections and retained cash across matched periods, rather than only higher revenue or adjusted earnings. Until then, monitor cash retention alongside operating growth rather than relax leverage discipline.
Basis: Research questions (CP-DR); Financial foundation (CP-1)

A time-aware downside begins with parts constraints or a utilization slowdown. The first operational break would be weaker completed-work throughput or slower component collections; receivables and inventory could then absorb cash before staffing and certification costs adjust. Following a large distribution, reduced cash retention would increase dependence on committed facilities and refinancing even if accounting margins remained high. Observable triggers are falling aftermarket organic sales, longer turnaround, renewed receivable and inventory absorption, and borrowing alongside further distributions. No peer-calibrated stress magnitude or numerical covenant threshold is established.
Basis: Research questions (CP-DR); Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Limitations
This is a completed, bounded comparison, not a blocked assessment for lack of candidate peers. StandardAero is usable for limited profitability context and HEICO for operating context, but neither establishes an aligned annual leverage series. The annual issuer data are retrospective and were published after the cutoff, whereas the retained peer disclosures were published before it. No calendarization, FX conversion, acquired-business pro forma or house earnings adjustment has been applied.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); Financial foundation (CP-1); Research questions (CP-DR)

The analysis does not establish a sector valuation premium, implied enterprise value, debt-coverage cushion, recovery value, security ranking or executable relative value. Missing peer cash-flow data also prevent conclusions about relative liquidity or sustainable deleveraging. The retained evidence supports issuer-specific servicing analysis and qualitative cross-checks, not an unrestricted peer ranking.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Research questions (CP-DR)
