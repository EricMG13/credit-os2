## Findings

### Bottom line
TransDigm’s earnings improvement is credible and supports stronger debt servicing, but it does not demonstrate deleveraging: acquisitions, higher debt and shareholder distributions prevent the operating gain from translating into a durable reduction in creditor risk. Reported EBITDA increased USD 665 million, or 21.12%, while operating cash flow increased USD 670 million, or 48.73%.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D01; calculation D02; calculation D04

The distinction is operating improvement versus financial policy. Gross leverage increased from 6.27x to 6.53x despite the earnings growth. Year-end net leverage of 4.88x becomes 6.02x after the October 2024 dividend, on Financial foundation’s unchanged annual EBITDA basis. The cash-flow acceleration is stronger than the earnings increase, but partly reflects less working-capital absorption rather than an equivalent step-up in recurring operating profitability.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D08; calculation B04

### Key findings
- Growth was not merely acquisition-driven. Management attributes USD 1,067 million of the USD 1,355 million sales increase to organic sales and USD 288 million to acquisition sales. Defense, commercial original-equipment and aftermarket demand all contributed, reducing dependence on a single end market for the annual improvement; the disclosure does not separate price from volume.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 45; calculation B01
- Unadjusted improvement is less dramatic than the adjusted margin narrative. Reported EBITDA increased USD 665 million, whereas EBITDA As Defined increased USD 778 million; the USD 113 million difference is the increase in add-backs. Compensation and acquisition-related adjustments account for most of that difference, so the adjusted growth rate should not be read as an equal improvement in cash earnings.
Basis: calculation D01; calculation D03; calculation B03; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 71
- Cash conversion improved, but retention did not follow. Free cash flow reached USD 1,880 million, up USD 644 million, while acquisition spending and annual dividend payments together left a USD 2,505 million deficit against that free cash flow, before other investing and financing items. The operating improvement therefore supports servicing capacity more clearly than debt repayment.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D05; calculation B05
- Coverage improvement is real but not a forecast of the next funding cycle. Reported EBITDA coverage of net cash interest rose from 2.71x to 3.29x even as net accrued interest expense increased. The paid-interest denominator benefits from receipts and timing; lower post-distribution cash and additional borrowing can weaken the relationship between historical cash coverage and future debt service.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 47–48; calculation D08

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest contrary evidence is broad organic growth, higher operating earnings and improved cash generation without a proportionate increase in capital spending. Management also reports positive fixed-cost absorption despite inflation. These facts argue against dismissing the earnings improvement as accounting presentation alone. A sustained record of cash retention, acquisition integration without rising adjustments, and stable conversion when receivables and inventory no longer provide a year-on-year tailwind would make the operating improvement a stronger deleveraging signal.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 45–51; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 56–57; calculation B04

The adverse pathway starts if production or flight-hour demand slows while inventory purchases or customer collection delays increase. Fixed-cost absorption then reverses first, operating cash falls faster than sales, and debt-funded acquisitions or distributions leave less room to absorb the working-capital outflow. The observable triggers are weaker organic channel growth, renewed receivable and inventory cash absorption, lower coverage and additional borrowing without a corresponding increase in retained cash. No numerical covenant or rating threshold is established here.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 45–51; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 54–57; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Limitations
- The annual variance establishes improvement over the prior year, not an exit-rate acceleration: no normalized quarterly comparison was inherited, so the conclusion cannot establish whether momentum strengthened or weakened late in the year.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1)
- The bridge separates the increase in adjustments from the increase in unadjusted EBITDA, but cannot attribute the entire operating increase between organic volume, pricing, acquisition profit and cost actions. Consequently, the durability of the earnings increment is less firmly established than its amount.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 45–51; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 71
- No dated prior earnings guidance, underwriting case or agency earnings benchmark is supplied. Therefore no beat, miss, guidance revision or forecast-validation conclusion is made; the prospective capital-spending statement is retained without inventing a prior target.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 56; Source readiness (CP-0)
