## Findings

### Bottom line

Liquidity is adequate for the twelve months to September 2025: after the October 2024 special dividend, accessible liquidity of USD 2,919 million plus recurring operating cash covers every scheduled cash use, so near-term default risk from liquidity is low for creditors.

The bridge starts at USD 7,267 million of reported cash liquidity, absorbs the USD 4,348 million dividend, USD 1,524 million of scheduled interest and repayment of the USD 650 million securitization, and ends at USD 3,550.1 million. The runway on that bridge is 23.46 months, but it is distorted by the one-off dividend; excluding it the year is cash-generative. The pinch points are October 2024 itself and the 2025-07-11 securitization maturity, after which any shortfall moves onto the revolver, where usage above USD 364 million springs a 7.50x net leverage test. Financial policy, not cash generation, is the variable that can erode this buffer.

### Key findings

- Opening liquidity is large on paper but more than half was already spoken for. Reported cash liquidity of USD 7,267 million (cash USD 6,261 million, revolver availability USD 843 million, securitization availability USD 163 million) fell to USD 2,919 million pro forma once the USD 4,348 million dividend and equivalents were paid on 2024-10-18, leaving cash of USD 1,913 million. The relevant buffer for creditors is the pro forma figure.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation L9

- Recurring operations fund all mandatory uses with room to spare. On an FY2024 run-rate, pre-interest, pre-tax operating cash of USD 4,044 million less a USD 302 million working-capital outflow covers scheduled interest of USD 1,524 million, cash taxes of USD 539 million, capex at the top of guidance of USD 277.9 million, term loan amortisation and the securitization repayment of USD 738 million, and pension and finance-lease payments; excluding the dividend, liquidity would rise to USD 7,898.1 million.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 56, 64–65; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation L1; calculation L4; calculation L10

- The interest step-up is the largest recurring pressure. Scheduled interest of USD 1,524 million is USD 366 million above the USD 1,158 million paid in FY2024 and rests on the issuer's Term SOFR assumption of 5.6% to 6.5%; it reduces the cash surplus but does not threaten the bridge.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 64–65; Fundamental credit (CP-2)

- The securitization is the only funding source with a maturity inside the horizon and is a pinch point rather than a gap. It was fully drawn to USD 650 million on 2024-10-16 and matures 2025-07-11; the bridge assumes full repayment, while the facility carries annual one-year extension rights absent termination events. Non-renewal shifts the funding need onto cash and the revolver.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 62–63; calculation L2

- Revolver access is real but conditional. Availability of USD 843 million is after USD 67 million of letters of credit; usage above USD 364 million triggers a 7.50x maximum net leverage test on EBITDA As Defined. Pro forma net leverage on that measure is 5.50x per Financial foundation, but the contractual definitions are not supplied, so headroom is not established.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 62, 65; Financial foundation (CP-1)

- The first quarter after the as-of date confirms the bridge direction. Cash liquidity was USD 3,308 million at 2024-12-28 (cash USD 2,459 million, revolver availability USD 849 million) after USD 4,396 million of dividend payments and USD 316 million of discretionary share repurchases, consistent with a post-dividend trough rather than a draining runway.
Basis: TDG_2024-12-28_10-Q_0001260221-25-000014_tdg-20241228.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 44; calculation L12

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate reading is that the issuer treats liquidity as distributable. Management states it may borrow for special dividends or share repurchases, ranks debt prepayment last, and repurchased USD 316 million of stock within weeks of a USD 4,348 million dividend. A further special dividend of the size of the September declaration would consume essentially all of the projected USD 3,550.1 million ending liquidity and force revolver use above USD 364 million, bringing the springing test into play. Operating risk is secondary: a USD 765 million EBITDA fall, the scale Fundamental credit uses to reach 2.00x interest cover, would still leave USD 2,785.1 million at year end if it passed fully into cash. The view would move to tight if another debt- or cash-funded special dividend were declared before July 2025, if the securitization were not extended at 2025-07-11, or if quarterly cash liquidity fell below the USD 2,919 million pro forma level; it would strengthen if cash were retained through FY2025.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 54, 56, 62; TDG_2024-12-28_10-Q_0001260221-25-000014_tdg-20241228.htm.jsonl, page 44; Fundamental credit (CP-2); calculation L13; calculation L14

### Limitations

- The operating inflow, working-capital outflow and cash taxes in the bridge are FY2024 actuals held flat, because no FY2025 revenue, EBITDA or tax guidance is supplied; the ending liquidity of USD 3,550.1 million is therefore an analyst estimate whose size, not its sign, would change with actual FY2025 trading.
- The bridge is annual and does not model intra-year timing of semi-annual note coupons or seasonal working capital, so the true intra-year trough after October 2024 may sit below the post-dividend USD 2,919 million level; the runway figure cannot locate it.
- Revolver access is counted in full on the issuer's disclosed availability, but the credit agreement is not supplied, so whether letters of credit count toward the USD 364 million springing threshold and how net debt is defined are not established; this could reduce the drawable amount before the 7.50x test applies and would weaken, not reverse, the adequate rating.
- The two figures for the October payment (USD 4,348 million and USD 4,396 million for the quarter, and dividend equivalents of USD 132 million against USD 131 million) are not reconciled; using the higher quarterly figure would lower ending liquidity by USD 48 million, which does not change the risk level.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 62–65; TDG_2024-12-28_10-Q_0001260221-25-000014_tdg-20241228.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 44; calculation L15

### Liquidity view

Overall, TransDigm has adequate near-term liquidity. Beginning accessible liquidity is USD 7,267 million reported and USD 2,919 million pro forma for the October 2024 dividend, while twelve-month cash uses excluding working capital are USD 7,458.9 million including that dividend. The key liquidity pressure is distribution policy, which matters because it converts the operating surplus into shareholder payments and pushes funding onto the revolver, and implies that liquidity depends on board restraint rather than on cash generation. Months to empty on the bridge is 23.46 months, distorted by the one-off dividend; excluding it the year is cash-generative. Further analysis requires the credit agreement, the securitization agreement and FY2025 guidance. Liquidity analysis completed with limitations. Liquidity risk level: Adequate. Key gaps: contractual revolver definitions, securitization renewal terms, FY2025 operating guidance.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56; calculation L4; calculation L7; calculation L11

### Liquidity risk assessment

Liquidity risk level: Adequate. The evidence is that accessible liquidity after the dividend (USD 2,919 million) plus a recurring operating surplus covers all scheduled twelve-month uses, and no term loan or note matures before November 2027. The risk mechanism that could change this is discretionary distribution funded from that buffer, followed by revolver drawings that spring the leverage test. The credit implication is low near-term default probability from liquidity, with monitoring focused on distributions, the securitization renewal and revolver usage rather than on operating burn.
