## Findings

### Bottom line

TransDigm's fundamental credit profile pairs a high-margin, cash-generative aerospace franchise with a financial policy that recycles growth into debt-funded distributions, so creditors face low near-term default risk but should expect leverage to be held rather than reduced.

Pro forma for the special dividend paid on 2024-10-18, net leverage is 6.02x on reported EBITDA, against 4.88x at year end before that payment, and 5.93x on the USD 3,871 million of earnings the evidence supports. The strongest support is an EBITDA As Defined margin of 52.56% from proprietary, aftermarket-heavy parts, converting into USD 1,880 million of free cash flow. The weakest dimension is financial policy: USD 6,368 million of special distributions were declared in FY2024 and debt prepayment ranks last in the use of cash. The primary downside path is slower commercial demand meeting scheduled interest of USD 1,524 million; revolver usage above USD 364 million is the trigger to watch.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Earnings quality (CP-1D); Business & governance (CP-1A); TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56

### Key findings

- The business is the strongest credit support because its pricing rests on certified, proprietary parts sold largely into the aftermarket. About 90% of sales are proprietary and about 55% aftermarket, the issuer declines lower-margin build-to-print work, and the EBITDA As Defined margin rose to 52.56% on organic growth of 16.2% across defense, commercial OEM and aftermarket. Once a part is certified on a platform it is costly for a customer to qualify a second source, so margins should hold through moderate volume pressure, which is what lets this capital structure service its debt.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 5; Business & governance (CP-1A); Earnings changes (CP-1B); Research questions (CP-DR)

- Financial policy is the weakest material dimension and, not operations, sets the leverage path. Management states it may increase borrowings for special dividends or share repurchases and ranks debt prepayment last in the use of excess cash; in FY2024 the board declared USD 6,368 million of special distributions, the September payment was 69% funded by USD 3,000 million of new secured debt, gross debt rose USD 5,130 million and buybacks of USD 316 million followed within the next quarter. Creditors should therefore treat EBITDA growth as distribution capacity, not deleveraging.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 56; Business & governance (CP-1A)

- Earnings should be underwritten on the supported figure, not the issuer measure. The evidence supports USD 3,871 million of FY2024 EBITDA against USD 4,173 million As Defined, because acquisition costs and stock compensation recur every period; on supported earnings pro forma net leverage is 5.93x against 5.50x on the issuer basis, a difference of 0.43x that a lender trusting the adjusted measure would wrongly treat as headroom.
Basis: Earnings quality (CP-1D)

- Cash conversion is real but debt service is about to absorb more of it. Free cash flow was USD 1,880 million, 49.31% of reported EBITDA, yet the issuer's schedule puts next-year interest at USD 1,524 million against USD 1,158 million paid in FY2024. Holding everything else equal, the USD 366 million step-up would cut free cash flow to about USD 1,514 million, and cover of scheduled interest by FY2024 reported EBITDA is 2.50x against 3.29x on cash paid.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation K1; calculation K2

- Liquidity is adequate after the special dividend and near-term refinancing risk is low. Pro forma cash liquidity of USD 2,919 million covers 1.92x one year of scheduled interest, and nothing matures before November 2027 except term loan amortisation of USD 93 million and the USD 650 million securitization due in July 2025. The refinancing exposure is concentrated later: FY2028 and FY2029 maturities total USD 11,405 million, 46.90% of the issuer's ladder, and must be rolled at market coupons.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation K3; calculation K4; calculation K5

- The policy raises loss severity for junior creditors more than default probability. Senior secured debt alone is 5.12x reported EBITDA after subordinated notes were replaced with secured debt and the September distribution was funded with secured debt, so each round of releveraging enlarges the claim ranking ahead of the subordinated notes without adding earnings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is that growth has so far absorbed the distributions. Reported EBITDA grew 21.12% in FY2024, gross debt to EBITDA As Defined moved only from 5.82x to 5.96x despite USD 6,368 million of declared distributions, about 77% of gross debt is fixed rate, and the term loans and notes carry no maintenance covenants, so a default trigger in the next two years is hard to construct. Management also states that operating cash flow and available borrowing capacity will meet interest and liquidity needs. On that reading the policy is leverage-neutral recycling of growth. The view would soften if the issuer stated a leverage ceiling or passed a year retaining cash and prepaying debt, or if supported EBITDA grew enough to bring pro forma net leverage on supported earnings back below the 5.17x of FY2023 without a further distribution. It would harden if quarterly sales growth fell below the 18.03% of the constructed fourth quarter, if another special dividend were declared while EBITDA As Defined fell, if revolver usage exceeded USD 364 million, or if a government pricing review cut defense margins.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Earnings changes (CP-1B); TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56

### Limitations

- Because the executed credit agreement and indentures are not in the documents, the conclusion that financial policy rather than contract bounds releveraging cannot be sized: if restricted-payment and secured-debt baskets prove tight, the financial-policy weakness would be less severe than stated here, and if loose, the recovery-dilution finding would be stronger.
- With no peer filings, ratings or market prices, the judgement that the business profile is strong rests on the issuer's own figures and descriptions; it may overstate standing against other aerospace component issuers, and the synthesis cannot say whether pro forma leverage near 6.02x is normal for the sector.
- No management revenue or EBITDA guidance is available, so the outlook that interest cover falls toward 2.50x is a mechanical extension of FY2024 earnings against the scheduled interest; growth at the FY2024 pace would offset much of it and soften the downside ranking of the interest step-up.
- The downside stress figures (a 20.06% EBITDA fall to reach 2.00x cover) are the analyst's own illustration, because the run settings supply no scenario assumptions; they show distance to stress, not a forecast, and change the downside narrative only in scale.

### Company, ownership and operating model

TransDigm Group Incorporated is a widely held, NYSE-listed Delaware holding company whose only material asset is TransDigm Inc., the borrower under the credit agreement and issuer of all notes; the parent and domestic restricted subsidiaries guarantee the notes. There is no controlling holder, so neither sponsor support nor a sponsor exit can be underwritten, and the board sets financial policy under public-equity incentives that include cash dividend equivalents for option holders. The operating model is a portfolio of acquired niche component businesses run on a stated value-driven strategy of winning profitable new business, controlling cost through productivity, and pricing products to reflect the value provided. For creditors this means cash-flow visibility is high at the operating level, while capital-structure outcomes depend on board choices.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 5

### Revenue durability and pricing power

Revenue is recurring at the part level but cyclical at the channel level. The aftermarket carries about 55% of sales and repeats with flight hours and fleet maintenance, defense about 40% follows budgets and outlays, and commercial OEM about 27% follows Boeing and Airbus build rates. Pricing power comes from sole-source certified parts, but two mechanisms limit it: the US government can review defense pricing for fairness and seek alternative sources, and the issuer's own risk factors say it may have to adjust prices to stay competitive. The credit implication is that revenue is durable against a moderate downturn but exposed to a simultaneous fall in air traffic and a defense pricing challenge.
Basis: Research questions (CP-DR); Business & governance (CP-1A); Earnings changes (CP-1B)

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins are high and expanding: gross margin was 58.8% and EBITDA As Defined margin 52.56%, with organic incremental margin estimated near 63.82% by Earnings changes. That operating leverage cuts both ways, so a volume decline would remove EBITDA faster than sales. Cost of sales grew 19.14% against sales growth of 20.58%, and acquired businesses contributed at a lower margin near 33.68%, so integration of the CPI Electron Device and Raptor businesses dilutes margins until the issuer's pricing and cost programme is applied. Margins look defensible under moderate volume pressure because proprietary pricing can be adjusted, but input-cost increases that cannot be recovered in pricing are a stated risk.
Basis: Earnings changes (CP-1B); TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 5

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

The business converts EBITDA to cash well: capex was 2.08% of revenue, free cash flow USD 1,880 million, and the gap between EBITDA As Defined and operating cash flow is fully explained by interest, tax and working capital. The FY2025 capex guide of 2.5% to 3.5% of sales implies USD 198.5 million to USD 277.9 million on FY2024 sales, a modest step-up. The binding claim on cash flow is interest, not capex: the scheduled USD 366 million increase in interest is larger than the whole capex budget. Free cash flow is therefore durable enough to service debt, but none of it is earmarked for debt reduction.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Earnings quality (CP-1D); TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 56; calculation K1; calculation K10; calculation K11

### Market frameworks, translated into credit terms

Competitive rivalry is low at the part level: the issuer describes fragmented niches with competitors ranging from divisions of large groups to small private firms, which supports margin durability and therefore debt capacity; the contrary statement that the industry is highly competitive means rivalry is a pricing ceiling rather than a volume threat. New entrants face certification cost and time, which protects aftermarket pricing and recovery value of the franchise. Substitution is slow because parts are certified to platforms, which supports revenue visibility over the FY2028 refinancing horizon. Buyer power is moderate: the top ten customers are about 42% of sales, and the US Department of Defense can contest pricing, which is the most direct threat to margins in the defense channel. Supplier power appears limited, but unrecovered raw-material cost is a stated risk. None of these forces is supported by data independent of the issuer.
Basis: Research questions (CP-DR); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Political and regulatory factors are material: FAA and European certification, export controls and defense pricing reviews can stop sales or cut margins in specific products, and an adverse review would hit the defense channel that grew most in FY2024. Economic factors are material through air traffic, OEM build rates and interest rates; about 23% of gross debt floats, so Term SOFR moves cash interest. Social and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver based on the documents.
Basis: Research questions (CP-DR); TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 55

In credit terms, the strengths are proprietary aftermarket pricing, cash conversion, a long maturity runway and no maintenance covenants. The weaknesses are debt-funded distributions, add-backs that overstate earnings, a rising secured share and an interest bill that steps up before earnings catch up. Opportunities to improve credit are full-year contributions from FY2024 acquisitions and any year in which cash is retained rather than distributed. Threats are an aftermarket slowdown, defense pricing reviews, OEM production problems and a further debt-funded special dividend.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Earnings quality (CP-1D); Research questions (CP-DR)

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale, competitive advantage, margin stability, cost and capex flexibility and free cash flow stability are the credit's supports, each grounded in the margins, capex intensity and conversion shown above. Business diversification is moderate: three channels and many platforms, but concentrated on aerospace and on two OEMs for new-build demand. Liquidity is adequate after the October payment rather than abundant. Ability to refinance depends on continued market access, since policy prevents deleveraging before the FY2028 and FY2029 maturities, and ratings and market prices are not available to test it. Financial policy and governance is weak for creditors. The trend is improving operations with deteriorating pro forma leverage. Missing datapoints are the debt documents' baskets and definitions, peer and rating evidence, maintenance capex and management guidance.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 months to 24 months, full-year contributions from the CPI Electron Device and Raptor acquisitions and continued defense and aftermarket demand support EBITDA, but cash interest rises toward the scheduled USD 1,524 million, capex steps up to the guided range, and the securitization must be renewed in July 2025; coverage is likely to fall unless growth holds near FY2024 rates. Over 3 years to 5 years the decisive item is refinancing USD 11,405 million in FY2028 and FY2029 at market coupons after several rounds of distribution-funded borrowing, which makes refinancing confidence dependent on market windows. The sector tailwind is commercial air traffic and defense outlays; the headwinds are OEM production problems cited by the issuer and defense pricing scrutiny. The company-specific headwind is the board's demonstrated willingness to re-lever.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Earnings changes (CP-1B); Research questions (CP-DR); calculation K3

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a slowdown in commercial aftermarket demand, already hinted at by a fourth-quarter organic increase of USD 49 million against a nine-month quarterly average of USD 68 million, combined with continued OEM production problems. The first operational break is EBITDA growth stalling while interest steps up by USD 366 million, which cuts free cash flow toward USD 1,514 million before any fall in earnings. If reported EBITDA fell 20.06%, or USD 765 million, to USD 3,048 million, cover of scheduled interest would reach 2.00x. With cash already reduced to USD 1,913 million after the 2024-10-18 payment, a shortfall or further acquisition would be met from the revolver, and usage above USD 364 million activates the 7.50x springing test. The refinancing consequence arrives in FY2028, when USD 6,637 million matures. Management levers are material: stopping distributions and acquisitions frees most of free cash flow, and capex is light. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through FY2027; the risk is refinancing terms and recovery dilution, not a near-term payment default.
Basis: Research questions (CP-DR); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation K1; calculation K2; calculation K6; calculation K7; calculation K8; calculation K9

Primary downside path: a commercial aftermarket slowdown that stalls EBITDA growth as interest steps up, forcing revolver use toward the springing test while distributions continue, ahead of the FY2028 refinancing wall.

### Overall credit view

Overall, TransDigm presents a strong business profile supported by proprietary, certified aftermarket parts and an EBITDA As Defined margin of 52.56%, but credit quality is constrained by a financial policy that funds special distributions with secured debt and ranks debt prepayment last. Cash-flow durability appears strong because capex is light and conversion was 49.31% of reported EBITDA, though interest will absorb more of it. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on continued market access and on EBITDA growth outrunning further distributions before the FY2028 and FY2029 maturities. How the business compares with typical leveraged aerospace issuers is not established because no peers are available. The primary downside path is the aftermarket slowdown described above. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, ratings, market prices, peer filings and management guidance. Fundamental credit analysis completed. Top risk: debt-funded distributions holding pro forma net leverage near 6.02x while interest steps up.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is pricing power on proprietary, certified parts: about 90% of sales are proprietary and the margin reached 52.56%, which keeps EBITDA high enough to service USD 24,880 million of debt. The second is cash conversion: capex of 2.08% of revenue leaves USD 1,880 million of free cash flow, enough to absorb the interest step-up. The third is the maturity runway: no term loan or note matures before November 2027 and there are no maintenance covenants, which keeps near-term default probability low.

The first weakness is financial policy: debt-funded special dividends and repurchases convert growth into distributions, holding pro forma net leverage at 6.02x. The second is earnings quality: the issuer measure exceeds supported earnings by USD 302 million, lifting apparent capacity. The third is the interest step-up to USD 1,524 million combined with the FY2028 and FY2029 wall, which leaves refinancing dependent on market access, and a rising secured share that dilutes recovery for subordinated notes.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Earnings quality (CP-1D)
