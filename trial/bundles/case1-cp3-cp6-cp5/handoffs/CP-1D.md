## Findings

### Bottom line

Evidence supports only USD 3,871 million of TransDigm's USD 4,173 million FY2024 adjusted EBITDA, so supported pro forma net leverage is 5.93x, not the issuer-basis 5.50x; creditors should underwrite on a figure close to reported EBITDA.

The USD 302 million gap is two recurring costs the issuer adds back: acquisition and integration costs of USD 70 million, which recur in every period and are paid in cash, and non-cash stock and deferred compensation of USD 217 million, a recurring cost of running the business. Refinancing costs of USD 58 million are supported as financing in nature; USD 15 million of other items is unresolved, which bounds the supported figure between USD 3,871 million and USD 3,886 million. Off-balance-sheet obligations are small: leases and pensions add USD 68 million to debt and 0.02x to leverage. Cash conversion of adjusted EBITDA was 49.01%, fully explained by interest, tax and working capital, so conversion is not the weakness; the weakness is that the issuer measure excludes real, repeating costs.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 70–72; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D1; calculation D2; calculation D4; calculation D11; calculation D20; calculation D33; calculation D38

### Key findings

- Acquisition and integration costs should not be added back, because they recur in every period tested and consume cash. The line was USD 18 million in FY2023 and USD 70 million in FY2024, and it appears in each quarter reported in the documents, including the first quarter of FY2025. The issuer itself states that the measure excludes integration cash expense that is a necessary element of certain of its acquisitions, and acquisitions absorbed USD 2,347 million of cash in FY2024. For a serial acquirer this cost is part of the business model, so removing it from earnings overstates debt capacity.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 70–71; TDG_2024-06-29_10-Q_0001260221-24-000069_tdg-20240629.htm.jsonl, page 72; TDG_2024-12-28_10-Q_0001260221-25-000014_tdg-20241228.htm.jsonl, page 54; Financial foundation (CP-1)

- Stock and deferred compensation is challenged, not accepted: it is genuinely non-cash, but it is the largest add-back at USD 217 million, recurs in every period and is part of the compensation cost of the business. The cash-flow reconciliation confirms the non-cash character, so it does not affect the cash test. It does affect leverage, because the restated figure is an earnings measure; carrying it at nil in the supported figure lifts pro forma net leverage by about 0.39x on its own relative to an add-back view.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 71; TDG_2024-06-29_10-Q_0001260221-24-000069_tdg-20240629.htm.jsonl, page 72; calculation D6; calculation D20; calculation D30

- Refinancing costs of USD 58 million are supported as an exclusion because they are debt-financing costs, largely write-offs of issuance costs, and interest is already outside EBITDA. They recur every year (USD 56 million in FY2023), so the creditor reading is that the issuer's frequent refinancing is a standing cost of its capital-structure strategy, belonging with fixed charges rather than operating earnings.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 71; TDG_2024-06-29_10-Q_0001260221-24-000069_tdg-20240629.htm.jsonl, page 72

- On supported earnings the leverage picture moves from the issuer's view to the reported view. Gross leverage is 6.43x against 5.96x on EBITDA As Defined; year-end net leverage is 4.81x on net debt of USD 18,619 million, which becomes 5.93x on USD 22,967 million pro forma for the 2024-10-18 dividend payment. Cover of cash interest is 3.34x. The 0.43x difference against the issuer-basis pro forma figure is the headroom a lender would wrongly assume by trusting the adjusted measure.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D18; calculation D19; calculation D20; calculation D30; calculation D35

- Cash conversion supports, rather than undermines, the earnings base. Operating cash flow was 49.01% of adjusted EBITDA, up from 40.50%, and the USD 2,128 million gap is exactly explained by the issuer's own reconciliation: interest of USD 1,246 million, current tax of USD 490 million, working capital of USD 272 million and the cash add-backs. Before interest and tax, conversion was 90.61%. The test also confirms that the acquisition-cost and other add-backs sit inside operating cash outflow, which is further evidence they are real costs.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 71; calculation D9; calculation D11; calculation D12; calculation D16; calculation D17

- Adjusted debt adds little. Finance leases (USD 262 million) and the securitization (USD 486 million) are already in reported debt; operating lease liabilities of USD 62 million and a net pension deficit of USD 6 million are the only additions, lifting debt to USD 24,948 million and gross leverage on supported earnings to 6.44x. The material debt-like item is the committed October distribution, already captured in pro forma net debt.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 155; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 171–172; calculation D22; calculation D26; calculation D38

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against this restatement is that EBITDA As Defined is the measure the revolving facility's springing test uses, and that stock compensation never touches cash, so a cash-flow lender could reasonably accept the USD 217 million add-back. Accepting it would put supported earnings near USD 4,088 million and pro forma net leverage near the issuer's 5.50x. A second point is that part of the acquisition line is inventory step-up of USD 21 million, a purchase-accounting charge with no cash cost; accepting it alone moves supported pro forma net leverage only to 5.90x. The view would change if the issuer showed acquisition and integration costs falling to negligible levels for several quarters without acquisitions slowing, or if equity-settled compensation were replaced so that the cost visibly left the earnings base. It would move the other way if acquisition costs keep rising with the larger deals (they rose 3.89 times year on year) or if the unresolved other items prove to be recurring cash costs such as withholding taxes on dividend equivalents, which grow with every special dividend.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 70–71; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D39; calculation D40; calculation D41; calculation D42

### Limitations

- The issuer does not break the USD 15 million of other items into foreign exchange, withholding tax, pension and deferred compensation components, so the supported EBITDA is a range of USD 3,871 million to USD 3,886 million and supported pro forma net leverage a range of 5.91x to 5.93x rather than a point.
- The credit agreement's definition of consolidated EBITDA is not supplied, so this restatement cannot say how much of the rejected or challenged add-backs lenders have contractually accepted; the supported figure is an analytical view of earnings quality only and does not measure covenant headroom.
- No rating-agency adjusted figures are in the documents, so the comparison of this adjusted debt and EBITDA with agency treatment rests on general practice, not on agency numbers; the divergence conclusion is therefore indicative.
- The acquisition-cost line mixes cash integration and deal costs with non-cash inventory step-up, and only the step-up amount is separately visible; if more of the line proved non-cash the cash-test support for rejecting it would weaken, though the recurrence finding would stand.
- Quarterly add-back amounts come from unaudited quarterly reports and a fourth quarter built by difference, so the quarter-level recurrence pattern is indicative; the annual pattern across FY2023 and FY2024 alone settles the recurrence verdicts.

### Downside path

The initiating condition is a slowdown in acquisition-led growth or a return to large deals. The first break is that integration and transaction costs, which rose from USD 18 million to USD 70 million, keep rising while the issuer continues to remove them from EBITDA As Defined, widening the gap between the contractual measure and supported earnings beyond USD 302 million. The financial transmission is that incurrence capacity and the springing test are measured on the wider figure, so debt-funded distributions can be sized on earnings the evidence does not support; on supported earnings pro forma net leverage is already 5.93x. The observable triggers are the quarterly acquisition-cost line (USD 13 million in the first quarter of FY2025 against USD 2 million a year earlier), add-backs as a share of EBITDA As Defined (8.63% in FY2024), and any further special dividend.
