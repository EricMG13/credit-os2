## Findings

### Bottom line

Sector evidence reachable for this run supports the issuer's own picture of fragmented, certification-protected niches with broad FY2024 demand recovery, but no independent source, peer figure or market trading level was found, so peer comparison and valuation context stay open for creditors.

Eight of twelve questions are answered, all from the issuer's own filings, so every answer is the issuer's characterisation rather than corroborated sector evidence. Organic sales grew 16.2% in FY2024 across defense, commercial OEM and aftermarket, but the fourth-quarter increments in commercial OEM and aftermarket ran below their nine-month quarterly averages. Regulation is both the entry barrier that protects pricing and the channel through which defense pricing can be challenged. How other producers define EBITDA, and where sector debt trades, are not established, so Peer benchmark and Fundamental credit cannot rely on this research for either.

### Key findings

- Competitive intensity looks low at the product level but is asserted, not corroborated. The issuer describes its niches as fragmented, with competitors ranging from divisions of large public groups to small private firms with one or two components, and says certification cost and time discourage customers from qualifying a second supplier. For creditors this is the mechanism behind margins that let the group carry high leverage: once a part is certified on a platform, aftermarket pricing is hard to contest. No other producer, trade body or rating agency was reachable to confirm it, and no competitor is named.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10

- Demand was broad in FY2024 but slowed late in the year in the two commercial channels. Organic sales rose 16.2%, with defense up 18.9%, commercial OEM up 20.4% and commercial aftermarket up 12.0%. By subtraction from the nine-month figures, the fourth-quarter organic increase was USD 51 million in commercial OEM against a nine-month quarterly average of USD 81 million, and USD 49 million in aftermarket against USD 68 million, while defense held at USD 119 million against USD 122.33 million. Aftermarket is the higher-margin channel, so a slower aftermarket increment is the earliest point at which EBITDA growth could fall behind the higher interest bill Financial foundation identifies for FY2025.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 45; TDG_2024-06-29_10-Q_0001260221-24-000069_tdg-20240629.htm.jsonl, page 51; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C7; calculation C8; calculation C9

- The business model is concentrated on proprietary, aftermarket-heavy parts, which raises margin resilience but also exposure to defense pricing scrutiny. The issuer estimates about 90% of FY2024 sales came from proprietary products and about 55% from the aftermarket, and it declines build-to-print work. Defense is about 40% of sales, and the issuer discloses that the U.S. Government may review its costs to judge whether pricing is fair and reasonable even on non-cost-based contracts, and could seek other suppliers. An adverse pricing review would hit the defense channel, which carried the largest organic increase in FY2024.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 9; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 27–28

- Regulation is answered from primary disclosure: aviation certification by the FAA, the European aviation safety agency and defense quality specifications, and export controls under ITAR and EAR. Loss of an approval or tighter oversight would raise costs or stop sales of affected parts; export denial would cut foreign defense and dual-use sales. The quarterly report before the as-of date states that risk factors were unchanged from the prior annual report, so these exposures were live through the year.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 10; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 28; TDG_2024-06-29_10-Q_0001260221-24-000069_tdg-20240629.htm.jsonl, page 75

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the benign competitive reading comes from the issuer itself. Its risk factors call the industry highly competitive and say it may have to adjust prices to stay competitive, which sits uneasily with the fragmented-niche description in its business section. Commercial OEM demand depends on Boeing and Airbus build rates, and the issuer cites regulatory and quality problems at Boeing's narrow-body MAX and wide-body Dreamliner programmes as having already hurt it; the fourth-quarter slowdown in commercial OEM increments is consistent with that. Defense pricing reviews are a standing threat to the channel that grew most. The view would change if independent sector data, such as peer filings, airline traffic statistics or agency sector reports, showed aftermarket growth slowing faster than the issuer's channel figures, or if peer filings showed competitors winning certification on the issuer's platforms. It would also change if peer EBITDA definitions proved narrower than EBITDA As Defined, which would make the issuer look more levered against peers than its own measure suggests.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 24; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 27–28; calculation C4; calculation C8

### Limitations

- No source independent of the issuer could be reached, so the competition, industry-structure and regulation answers are the issuer's own characterisation; they support a direction of view, not a corroborated sector fact, and carry lower weight in Fundamental credit than an independent source would.
- The annual report was published on 2024-11-07, after the as-of date; the full-year demand figures and the business-section description of competition rest on it, while the nine-month demand figures and the unchanged-risk-factor statement come from the June 2024 quarterly report filed before the as-of date. If only pre-cutoff documents are admitted, the demand answer rests on nine months and the fourth-quarter slowdown finding falls away.
- The fourth-quarter increments are derived by subtracting nine-month from full-year organic increases, and the two periods end on different weekdays in the prior year; the slowdown finding is therefore indicative of direction, not an exact quarterly growth rate.
- Public registrant lookups were refused, so no peer candidate, peer EBITDA definition or peer figure exists; this leaves the metric-definition and valuation answers unresolved and the peer candidate tables empty.
