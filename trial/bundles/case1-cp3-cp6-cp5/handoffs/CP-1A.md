## Findings

### Bottom line

TransDigm is a widely held aerospace components issuer whose board treats debt-funded special dividends as repeatable policy, so creditors should underwrite gross debt near 5.96x EBITDA As Defined rather than deleveraging; governance and financial-policy risk is High.

The business carries that leverage: FY2024 net sales were USD 7,940 million and EBITDA As Defined USD 4,173 million at a 52.56% margin, with 55% of sales from the aftermarket and EBITDA As Defined covering net interest 3.24x. Policy absorbs the cushion. The board declared USD 6,368 million of special distributions in FY2024, 1.53x EBITDA As Defined, and USD 3,000 million of new senior secured debt funded 69% of the September payment. Gross carrying debt rose USD 5,130 million to USD 24,880 million. After the October 2024 payment the annual report puts pro forma cash liquidity at USD 2,919 million against USD 7,267 million reported. No term loan or note matures before November 2027, so the risk is recovery dilution and market dependence, not near-term default.

### Key findings

- The franchise is the reason the capital structure works. About 55% of FY2024 net sales came from the aftermarket and about 90% from proprietary products, which management says have historically produced higher and more stable gross profit than OEM sales; the EBITDA As Defined margin of 52.56% and net interest cover of 3.24x are the cash engine that services USD 24,880 million of carrying debt. Concentration is moderate: the top ten customers were about 42% of sales and none exceeded 10%.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–9; calculation C2; calculation C20; calculation C3

- Financial policy, not operations, sets creditor risk. The annual report ranks debt prepayment fourth and last in the use of excess cash, behind capex, acquisitions and special dividends or buybacks, and says the company may borrow for special dividends. FY2024 bore that out with two special dividends totalling USD 6,368 million declared, the second funded largely by USD 3,000 million of new senior secured debt issued the same day it was declared.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 54–56; calculation C8; calculation C10

- Cash uses ran well ahead of operating cash flow, and debt filled the gap. FY2024 dividends paid of USD 2,038 million, acquisitions of USD 2,347 million and capex of USD 165 million totalled USD 4,550 million against operating cash flow of USD 2,045 million, a USD 2,505 million shortfall; gross carrying debt rose USD 5,130 million and the stockholders' deficit widened by USD 4,306 million to USD 6,290 million.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 56–57; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 120; calculation C11; calculation C12; calculation C5; calculation C17

- The capital structure drifted towards secured debt in FY2024, which weakens the position of the remaining subordinated notes. USD 550 million of 2027 subordinated notes were redeemed with a tack-on to the 2029 secured notes, and the September 2024 distribution was funded with USD 3,000 million of new secured debt. Each step lowers refinancing risk on the retired paper but enlarges the secured claim pool ranking ahead of subordinated holders, who are also structurally subordinated to non-guarantor liabilities.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 53–54; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 57–58

- There is no controlling shareholder, so no sponsor exit or sponsor support can be underwritten; incentives run through the board and option holders. Management option holders received dividend equivalents of USD 233 million on FY2024 declarations, including USD 131 million paid in October 2024, which ties executive cash pay to special dividends rather than to debt reduction.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest counterargument is that EBITDA growth has so far absorbed the distributions. EBITDA As Defined grew USD 778 million in FY2024, operating cash flow rose to USD 2,045 million from USD 1,375 million, and gross carrying debt to EBITDA As Defined moved only from 5.82x to 5.96x despite USD 6,368 million of declared distributions. No term loan or note matures before November 2027, about 77% of gross debt was fixed rate at year-end, and the term loans and indentures carry no maintenance covenants, so near-term default probability is low. On that reading the policy is leverage-neutral recycling of growth, not deterioration. The view would soften if the board stated a leverage ceiling or a year passed with cash retained and debt prepaid, and it would harden if a special dividend were declared while EBITDA As Defined fell, if secured issuance continued to displace subordinated debt, or if the revolver were drawn above 40% of commitments, which brings the 7.50x springing test into force.

### Limitations

- The finding that no holder controls the board rests on the annual report's holder-of-record count and the absence of any controlling-holder disclosure, because the proxy statement with large holdings and board composition was not read; a large holder with board influence would shift the policy driver in the ownership map from the board alone to that holder.
- The High risk level is a willingness conclusion only: how much more can legally be distributed or secured is not established because restricted-payment and investment baskets are not stated in the documents, so the size of the next distribution cannot be bounded and the conclusion that the pattern is repeatable says nothing about its scale.
- Acquisition appetite is scored as mixed rather than adverse because purchase multiples and acquired EBITDA per deal are not disclosed; if FY2024 acquisitions were bought at multiples above group leverage, the acquisition channel would add to, not offset, the releveraging.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

There is no single acquisition or sponsor deal in front of creditors; the transaction is FY2024's sequence of refinancings, debt-funded acquisitions and two special dividends, which the annual report describes as about USD 21,000 million of debt financing transactions. New money came in two blocks. In November 2023 the company raised USD 2,000 million, split between the 2031 secured notes and Tranche J term loans, which part-funded the USD 1,385 million CPI Electron Device acquisition. In September 2024 it raised USD 3,000 million, split between USD 1,500 million of 6.00% secured notes due January 2033 and USD 1,500 million of Tranche L term loans due January 2032, concurrently with the USD 75.00 per share special dividend.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 53–54

The rest was liability management on market terms with no evidence of non-pro-rata or priming treatment. In February 2024 the USD 4,400 million 2026 secured notes were refinanced with the 2029 and 2032 secured notes, and the revolver was extended to February 2029 with commitments raised from USD 810 million to USD 910 million. In March and June 2024 term loans were repriced and extended into Tranches J and K, and USD 550 million of 2027 subordinated notes were redeemed with a tack-on to the 2029 secured notes. The securitization facility was raised to USD 650 million. The September dividend was paid on 18 October 2024: USD 4,216 million of dividend and USD 131 million of option equivalents, against the annual report's figure of about USD 4,348 million.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 53–54; TDG_2024-12-28_10-Q_0001260221-25-000014_tdg-20241228.htm.jsonl, page 44; calculation C15

### How the policy breaks

The initiating condition would be a fall in commercial air travel or a delay in US defense outlays, since the commercial aftermarket was about 31% and defense about 40% of FY2024 sales. The first operational break is a mix shift away from the higher-margin aftermarket, which compresses EBITDA As Defined while carrying debt and USD 1,286 million of annual net interest stay fixed. Gross leverage then rises from 5.96x with no contractual brake from maintenance covenants unless revolver usage exceeds USD 364 million, when the 7.50x test applies. The refinancing consequence arrives from November 2027, when the first notes and term loans must be rolled at market coupons after several years of distribution-funded debt growth. The observable triggers are the next special dividend or secured issuance, revolver drawings above that threshold, and a decline in aftermarket share.

### Overall governance view

Overall, TransDigm presents High governance and financial-policy risk. The key supported behaviour is the September 2024 special dividend funded by USD 3,000 million of new senior secured debt, following a USD 2,020 million cash-funded special dividend in November 2023, which matters because the stated use of excess cash ranks debt reduction last and therefore converts EBITDA growth into distributions; for creditors this means leverage is managed to a level rather than reduced, secured claims grow ahead of subordinated holders, and recovery cushion thins with each round. Countervailing evidence is strong cash generation, audited and segment-level reporting, and a maturity profile with nothing due before November 2027. Further analysis requires restricted-payment and secured-debt capacity from the executed documents and holder and board detail from the proxy statement. The governance assessment is completed with limitations at risk level High.
