Scope is the FY2024 against FY2023 earnings, cash-flow, leverage and coverage delta, its drivers (organic, acquisition, segment and add-back), a constructed fourth-quarter comparison, comparability events and the monitoring signals that follow.

## Findings

### Bottom line

Earnings improved strongly and broadly in FY2024, with reported EBITDA up 21.12% on mostly organic growth, but creditors gained little because debt-funded distributions lifted pro forma net leverage to 6.02x from 5.17x.

The signal is reliable: growth came from defense, commercial OEM and aftermarket volume across both aerospace segments, acquisitions explain only about USD 97 million of the USD 778 million rise in EBITDA As Defined, and free cash flow rose 52.1%. Two qualifications matter. Growth slowed in the constructed fourth quarter, and reported-EBITDA margin fell in that quarter as add-backs doubled. And flat cash interest of USD 1,158 million flatters the 3.29x coverage, because interest expense in the fourth quarter alone was USD 343 million. Year-end net leverage of 4.88x on net debt of USD 18,619 million becomes 6.02x on USD 22,967 million pro forma for the 2024-10-18 dividend payment.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 45–51; TDG_2024-06-29_10-Q_0001260221-24-000069_tdg-20240629.htm.jsonl, pages 43–44; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation E4; calculation E14; calculation E47; calculation E75

### Key findings

- The earnings improvement is organic and volume-led, which makes it the strongest part of the credit. Organic sales rose USD 1,067 million (16.2%) of the USD 1,355 million total increase, led by defense (USD 486 million, 18.9%), commercial OEM (USD 294 million, 20.4%) and commercial aftermarket (USD 253 million, 12.0%); acquisition sales added USD 288 million. Because defense and aftermarket are the highest-margin, least discretionary channels, the earnings base is less exposed to one cycle than a single-channel supplier would be.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 45; calculation E1; calculation E2

- Operating leverage is high and was real in the year, so EBITDA is likely to grow faster than sales while volumes hold, and to fall faster if they turn. EBITDA As Defined rose USD 778 million on USD 1,355 million of extra sales, an incremental margin of 57.42%; on the organic side the approximate figure is 63.82%, while acquired businesses contributed at about 33.68%. The issuer attributes the margin gain to its pricing and cost strategy and to fixed overhead spread over higher volume; that is management's explanation, not an audited fact.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 46–51; calculation E45; calculation E48; calculation E49

- The two EBITDA measures diverge, and the gap widened, so the issuer-adjusted figure overstates cash earnings by a growing amount. The add-backs rose USD 113 million to USD 360 million, chiefly non-cash stock and deferred compensation, up USD 60 million (38.22%) because a higher share price raised option fair values, and acquisition costs, up USD 52 million. Reported EBITDA margin rose only 0.21 pp to 48.02%, against 1.00 pp to 52.56% on EBITDA As Defined.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47; TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 71; calculation E68; calculation E70; calculation E72; calculation E73

- Growth slowed into year end, which is the first item to watch. Constructed fourth-quarter sales rose 18.03% and EBITDA As Defined 19.42%, against 21.57% and 24.30% for the first nine months. Fourth-quarter EBITDA As Defined margin of 52.61% was below the third quarter's 53.3% as lower-margin acquisitions were consolidated, and reported-EBITDA margin fell to 47.62% from 49.19% a year earlier because fourth-quarter add-backs rose to USD 109 million from USD 52 million.
Basis: TDG_2024-06-29_10-Q_0001260221-24-000069_tdg-20240629.htm.jsonl, pages 43–44; calculation E53; calculation E57; calculation E58; calculation E64; calculation E66; calculation E79; calculation E81

- Cash conversion improved, but none of the gain reached creditors. Operating cash flow rose USD 670 million and free cash flow USD 644 million to USD 1,880 million, with conversion of reported EBITDA up 10.05 pp to 49.31%, helped by a smaller working-capital outflow; cash taxes doubled to USD 539 million. Total debt rose USD 5,130 million to USD 24,880 million, and gross leverage rose 0.26x to 6.53x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation E13; calculation E15; calculation E32; calculation E37; calculation E74

- Coverage improvement is partly timing. Cover of cash interest by reported EBITDA rose 0.58x to 3.29x because cash interest was flat (down USD 2 million) while interest expense rose USD 122 million to USD 1,286 million on higher Term SOFR and more borrowing; the fourth quarter's USD 343 million of interest expense, against USD 292 million a year earlier, shows the run-rate climbing before the September 2024 issuance carries a full year.
Basis: TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 47–48; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation E27; calculation E29; calculation E43; calculation E75; calculation E76

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious read is that the earnings delta is broad, organic and still running near 20% in the fourth quarter, and that on the issuer-adjusted measure, the one the revolver test uses, net leverage is 5.50x pro forma for the 2024-10-18 payment. On that reading, another year of growth at the FY2024 incremental margin would take back most of the leverage added by the September distribution without any debt reduction. That view would win if fiscal 2025 quarters show EBITDA As Defined growth holding near the nine-month pace of 24.30% while interest expense stabilises, and if no further special dividend is declared. It would lose if quarterly growth continues to fade below the 18.03% sales growth of the constructed fourth quarter, if add-backs keep rising faster than EBITDA, or if the issuer again re-levers to fund a distribution, as it did twice in FY2024. The view would also change if a later filing restated FY2024 figures or reclassified refinancing costs between quarters.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); TDG_2024-09-30_10-K_0001260221-24-000083_tdg-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 54; calculation E53; calculation E79

### Limitations

- The fourth quarter is built by subtracting nine-month unaudited figures from audited full-year figures, so the deceleration finding is indicative; a restatement or reclassification between quarters, such as the refinancing costs that are larger for nine months than for the full year, would move the fourth-quarter margins.
- No management revenue or EBITDA guidance, prior house base case or rating-agency case is in the documents, so the year cannot be called a beat or a miss and the comparison is limited to the prior year and the nine-month run-rate.
- The first quarter of FY2024 and the first half were not separately rebuilt, so the intra-year view rests on two points (nine months and the constructed fourth quarter) and cannot show whether the slowdown started earlier.
- The acquisition contribution to EBITDA As Defined is the issuer's approximate figure for the full year and is not given for the fourth quarter, so the organic incremental margin and the attribution of the fourth-quarter margin dip to acquisitions are estimates.
